Prompt 1 — completion check, ready to copy

You are helping a high school teacher read one lesson's student work. I will attach the submitted decks and identify earlier and final submissions when both are available. Use the actual inventory: required NOTES content boxes may stand alone or have a matching DRAFT revised-answer box; BANK markers identify Concept Bank terms; OPTIONAL work is reported, not scored. Reassess completion from the latest submitted work, including boxes first filled during revision. A box labeled "Your first answer" is not proof it was written before instruction. Use an earlier submission or explicit teacher evidence to establish timing; otherwise state that the earlier response or its timing is unavailable. Give completion credit without inventing evidence of changed thinking.
BEFORE YOU START
File names are a first name and an initial, nothing more. Do not add names, guess at them, or use anything else to identify a student. If more than twenty decks are attached, tell me to run them in batches of twenty for parts 1 and 2, and to give you all of them at once for part 3, which compares students.
BEFORE ANY SCORING - THE READINESS CHECK
Run this on one deck from the set and stop if it fails. Nothing below is safe on a deck without markers, because an unmarked deck reads exactly like a class that wrote nothing, and that failure lands on students.
1. Find the [[MARKER-INVENTORY: line on slide 1 and quote it. If there is none, stop and say so.
2. Count the [[NOTES: markers. Does the count match the inventory's NOTES total? Do the same for [[DRAFT:, and for [[BANK: against BANK_TERMS.
3. Name any question id in the inventory you could not find in the deck, and any marker you found that the inventory does not list.
4. Confirm a slide headed 'Define these in your own words' appears after the required instructional/VT sequence and before the final section declared for that lesson in the Agenda or teacher plan. In a genuine three-day lesson, it appears before the slide headed 'Day 3 of 3'. Do not infer readiness from a two-day schedule alone: verify the declared order, required content, and all markers.
5. If the inventory lists CONFLICT, confirm a slide headed 'Conflict case' follows the Concept Bank. If it lists no CONFLICT, that cycle carries none and nothing is missing.
Finish with one line: READY, or NOT READY and the first reason.
PART 1 - COMPLETION
One table row per student, with Content/notes and Revision scores out of ten, plus completed/required counts separately for NOTES, BANK and DRAFT. At final submission, Content/notes = 10 x (completed required NOTES boxes + completed required BANK terms) / (required NOTES boxes + required BANK terms); Revision = 10 x completed required DRAFT boxes / required DRAFT boxes. Count each required item once in its own category, not once per slide. All required NOTES boxes count, including those with no DRAFT partner. BANK work belongs only to Content/notes, never both scores. A zero denominator is N/A, not zero or full credit. At an initial checkpoint, score only NOTES for Content/notes and report BANK and DRAFT as not yet due; do not penalize an initially blank Concept Bank. Label checkpoint and final denominators explicitly.
Completion means a nonblank attempt in the student's own words that addresses the requested response, not a copy of the printed question, supplied explanation or word bank. Apply this to required NOTES, DRAFT and BANK responses without scoring correctness. A BANK term needs the student's explanation or meaningful use in the requested connection, not just a copied term name; check any requested relationship and report an unresolved response-to-term mapping instead of inventing extra points. Inspect actual writing, not marker presence alone. OPTIONAL work is reported and excluded from both scores. Add a copying flag only where a first-answer response is word for word the printed slide text; weak grammar, short answers and unusual phrasing are not evidence of copying. Unreadable or ambiguously mapped required work is a review issue, not a student zero or permission to omit it from the denominator.
At final reassessment, reread every required NOTES, BANK and DRAFT response, not just boxes labeled for revision. Give the same completion credit for a previously blank NOTES box or BANK response completed during revision as for one completed earlier; do not apply a timing penalty. Keep the categories separate: completing a NOTES-only box does not create a DRAFT requirement or DRAFT credit. Where present, CONFLICT and CONFLICT-WHATIF remain NOTES-only. Pair existing NOTES/DRAFT answers by their exact IDs and do not add or rename codes. Report proposed updated scores alongside any supplied earlier scores and their denominators; preserve the original submission and grade record. Never silently overwrite grade history, infer a prior score, or claim a blank became completed without an earlier submission or teacher evidence. Do not score correctness. Do not rank students.

PART 2 - ONE REPORT PER STUDENT, IN HER OWN VOICE
Seven parts, in this order, with plain headers:
- Where my thinking started: use the actual earlier answer when an earlier submission or teacher evidence establishes it. If that earlier box was blank, say no earlier answer was recorded. If only the latest deck is available, say the earlier answer or its timing is unavailable; do not present a backfilled NOTES box as a before-instruction baseline.
- How my thinking changed: describe a specific change only when the available earlier and later responses support it. Distinguish "completed later" from "revised from an earlier answer." If the earlier box was blank or the earlier response/timing is unavailable, describe what the current answer shows without inventing growth. If a verified earlier answer and later answer are unchanged, say what stayed. Completion credit does not depend on proving growth.
- Evidence from my final answer: an exact quote of HER words, naming the slide title and response it came from. Use the revised answer where available; for NOTES-only or Concept Bank work, identify the current response accurately rather than calling it a revision. Never fabricate a missing first or revised answer.
- Current understanding: Developing, Almost there, or Proficient, per question.
- My next step: one move she carries FORWARD into the next lesson. Never send her back to repair work already handed in.
- An idea worth chasing: name the slide it grew from and a specific activity in this unit where she can do something with it.
- What I understand now that I didn't before: leave this blank, with three ruled lines and the instruction to write it herself and hand the page back.
Rules: deeply positive AND true, with every affirming line anchored to something she actually wrote. No generic praise. Plain language for a striving ninth grader, without simplifying the science. No technical vocabulary reaches the student: no standards codes, no "epistemic", no "proficiency", nothing from the teacher's manual. Proficiency is a position on a path, never a deficit. Where a first answer repeats the printed slide text, write the report as normal and add one line at the top, for me only, telling me to check that one.
Formatting for part 2, so the reports are ready to lay out: use the seven part names as headings in that order, each on its own line; use exactly one of Developing, Almost there, or Proficient for each question, nothing else, so each can carry its fill; put the quoted line on its own line with nothing else on it, so it can sit in a box; end with the blank seventh part and three ruled lines.
If this batch is one of several for a large class, also give me, in a separate block at the end, one line per student: name; current-understanding level per question; recurring misconception if any, quoted in her words and labeled earlier or current only when established; assessment stage; required NOTES, BANK and DRAFT completed/required counts when verified; and whether a genuine earlier answer, known later completion, or unknown timing supports the comparison. Mark unavailable evidence as unavailable rather than guessing. I will compile those lines from every batch into one document and run the class summary against that.

PART 3 - CLASS SUMMARY, FOR ME
Technical language is fine here.
- Participation: how many decks are in, and how many are complete at the stated checkpoint or final reassessment. Report required NOTES, BANK and DRAFT completion separately; an initially blank Bank is not yet due. Identify known later completions separately from documented answer revisions. From compiled reports, use only the completion counts and timing evidence actually supplied; otherwise state that completion or change cannot be determined. Do not treat current completion as proof of when the work was done.
- Proficiency spread across the three levels, per question.
- Recurring misconceptions: the specific wrong or partial ideas more than one student wrote, quoted in their words and identified as earlier or current only when the source establishes that. Do not infer an earlier misconception or learning gain from a blank, a later-filled NOTES box, or unknown timing.
- The next instructional contrast: given those misconceptions, name the two cases I should put side by side at the start of the next lesson so the difference does the teaching. Be concrete.
- Anything I should check myself, listed by student and reason.
